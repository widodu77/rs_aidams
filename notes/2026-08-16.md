# 2026-08-16 — Phase G: the paper

Phase F closed yesterday. Everything from here is communication, not experiment. No new runs, no new
numbers; if a number in the paper does not already exist in `results/`, it does not go in.

## The structural decision

I set out to write this as a chronology, which is how the notes read: set out to build a gate, got a
null, diagnosed it, fixed it, got a cliff. That is honest and it is also the wrong shape for four
pages. A chronology spends its space on the order things happened in, and the order is not the
contribution.

The paper is three findings with equal billing instead:

1. **The prize is real and no prompt collects it.** Oracle at 92.7% @ 99.5 tokens against
   always-think's 89.5% @ 264.1, and three prompt wordings that all sit above 96% think rate.
2. **λ cancels out of the GRPO update.** The derivation written out in full, then the numerical
   verification on 344 logged steps.
3. **Repairing the rollout exposes a cliff.** The sweep table, the argmin argument, and then what
   survives: chain of thought before a tool call is purchasable.

The token saving is section 6, deliberately not section 1. It is the most immediately impressive
number in the project and it is also the most confounded, and leading with it would invite exactly
the misreading the notes have been guarding against since Phase E. The mechanism carries the weight;
the saving is what falls out of it.

## What went in that was not in the notes

**A related-work section.** The notes never had one, because the notes are a lab record and the
paper is an argument. Writing it forced a claim I had not made explicitly before: the length-penalty
literature (Arora & Zanette, L1) is about the *length* of reasoning on problems that need some,
whereas this study is about whether reasoning happens *at all* on problems that need none. Those are
adjacent questions and the second is the one where the cancellation bites. Worth keeping.

**The relationship to Dr. GRPO.** Liu et al. criticised GRPO's std normaliser as a source of bias.
That is close enough to this finding to need a sentence distinguishing them, and the distinction is
real: bias skews an update, whereas for a length-penalised reward the same normaliser removes the
swept variable entirely. Their remedy (drop the division) would in fact fix cancellation #1. It
would not fix #2, because #2 is about the location of the optimum, not the size of the step.

## Two things I got wrong while drafting, both caught by the figure

**The run count.** The paper says "twelve trained runs" throughout, which is the Phase E count: five
paired, five unpaired, two normaliser variants. Then the frontier figure printed a legend saying
thirteen, because `paired_isoff_lam2_0` — the control built later for the gate comparison — also puts
the decision in the prompt and belongs in that cloud. Both numbers are right about different sets.
Fixed by making the figure say thirteen and the caption name the split. **The figure caught a
counting error the prose had been carrying silently.** That is an argument for generating figures
from the result files rather than drawing them.

**The training-time think rate.** Figure 2 plots think rate during training and the prompt-decision
runs sit flat at 0.5, not at 0.98. For a moment I read that as a contradiction with the eval tables.
It is not: half of every paired group is *forced* direct, so 0.5 is a floor imposed by the rollout,
and those same policies still reason on 98% of items at evaluation because the force is gone.
Training-time and eval-time think rate are different quantities and the paper now says so in the
caption. Anyone reading the raw logs without that sentence would draw the wrong conclusion.

## Build

`paper/main.tex`, four pages exactly, TinyTeX locally (`pdflatex; bibtex; pdflatex; pdflatex`).
`paper/make_figures.py` reads `results/pareto.json` and `runs/*/log_history.json` and writes both
figures, so no figure can disagree with a table. TinyTeX was missing the Courier metrics that
`\texttt` under `times` needs; `tlmgr install courier` fixed it.

The programme rule is that LLM use is allowed only if every concept and line can be explained, and
labelled. Section 10 states it. The part that has to be genuinely mine on demand is the derivation
in section 4, specifically why `std(r) = λ·std(t)/B` when correctness is constant, and what the
ε → 0 limit is doing. Sitting with that before submission, not after.
