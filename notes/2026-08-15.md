# 2026-08-15 — Phase E result: the frontier is a point cloud

The full evaluation is in. Twelve trained policies, three fixed-policy anchors, one oracle bound,
all on the same 248 held-out items, all scored by BFCL's AST checker through the same generation
path. **No policy learned to gate, at any λ, under either sampling scheme, under either
normaliser.** The result is a null — but a mechanistic one, with the cancellation identity of
`notes/2026-08-13.md` explaining it and a measured ceiling showing what was forfeited.

Per-category think rate is 90–100% across every policy and every category, so the break-even

The ordering was never approached.

**This is the reason the significance module exists**, and it should be run before any table in the
write-up implies an ordering. A frontier plotted from these numbers would show a trend that the
data does not contain.

## Pairing backfired, significantly, and the mechanism was predicted

The one large, unambiguous effect: **paired λ=2.0 costs +32.9 tokens/item [+23.1, +43.0] against
the identical unpaired run**, with no accuracy difference (p=0.607). The ordering holds for all
five λ — paired 259–272 tokens, unpaired 224–239, ten runs, no overlap.

The mechanism was flagged before the sweep and is now confirmed. `enable_thinking=False` prefills
`<think></think>` into the **prompt**, so a direct rollout's completion never contains the decision
not to think — it begins after that decision was made for it. Reinforcing direct rollouts therefore
raises their probability under a prompt that is never deployed, while thinking rollouts are
reinforced under the prompt that *is*. Forcing exploration into the group biased the deployed policy
**toward** reasoning.

That is a genuine finding, not just a failed experiment: the standard trick for injecting
exploration into GRPO — sample the alternative mode by changing the prompt — is invalid whenever
the mode is expressed *in the prompt* rather than in the completion. The rollouts are off-policy
with respect to deployment, and the direction of the bias is not obvious in advance. Train-time
`think_rate` cannot reveal it, because pairing pins that quantity at 0.5 by construction. Only the
eval could show it.

## λ's one significant length effect runs backwards

Within the unpaired sweep, λ=0.5 is **10.7 tokens shorter** than λ=2.0 [−18.7, −3.6] — a
significant difference in the wrong direction. A four-fold increase in the length penalty produced
*longer* output. Combined with the non-monotone ordering across all five λ in both sweeps, this is
not a weak λ response; it is the absence of one, exactly as the cancellation identity predicts.

## What this study establishes

1. **The opportunity is real and large.** Oracle 92.7% @ 99.5 tokens vs always-think 89.5% @ 264.
   Perfect per-item gating is simultaneously more accurate and 2.7× cheaper.
2. **Prompting cannot reach it.** The adaptive prompt reasons on 96.8% of items.
3. **Length-penalised GRPO cannot reach it, for a structural reason, not a tuning one.** Under
   group-standardised advantages λ cancels exactly whenever correctness and format are constant
   within the normalising set — 68.8% of steps at one group per batch, identity exact to 9.7e-06
   across 344 groups and five λ spanning 40×.
4. **Both natural repairs fail, measurably and for different reasons.** Forced pairing biases the
   deployed policy toward reasoning (+32.9 tokens, p<0.05 on cost). Batch normalisation restores
   λ's meaning on 61% of steps but at a magnitude too small to move the policy, and still cancels
   on the other 39%.

**The claim to defend is about this objective under this optimiser**, not about the problem being
unsolvable. Limits to state plainly: 100 optimiser steps, Qwen3-1.7B, LoRA r=32, one task family,
a single seed per configuration.

## Then the fix worked

Everything above stands. What follows was run afterwards and changes the conclusion.

The Phase E diagnosis said the pairing failed because `enable_thinking=False` puts the decision in
the **prompt**, leaving it outside the completion the policy is trained on. `--gate-rollouts`
corrects that: one prompt for the whole group, rendered exactly as at evaluation, and the direct
half forced by prepending Qwen3's own empty-think marker to its **completion**. The parser already
scores an empty think block as `did_think=False` with zero think tokens, so the reward needed no
change. Control run: the old pairing at the same lambda with the importance-sampling correction
also disabled, since the forced tokens have no sampled logprob.

**Training-time behaviour, identical in every respect but one:**

| run | decision in | think tok first10 → last10 | think rate last10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `paired_isoff_lam2_0` | prompt | 111.6 → 90.0 | 0.49 |
| `gate_lam2_0` | **completion** | 90.6 → **0.0** | **0.00** |

The gate run trains normally until step ~23, then goes fully no-think and stays there. From that
point `frac_reward_zero_std` is 1 on nearly every step, loss and grad_norm are exactly 0, and
runtime falls from ~1000 s to 605 s because completions got so short. **The unforced half stopped
reasoning too**, which is the policy changing rather than the forcing being read back.

**At evaluation, on the held-out 248:**

| policy | acc | tokens | think rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| never (prompted) | 71.4% | 51.4 | 0% |
| always | 89.5% | 264.1 | 98.4% |
| adaptive prompt | 89.1% | 280.6 | 96.8% |
| decision=prompt λ=2.0 | 89.5% | 266.8 | 98.4% |
| **decision=completion λ=2.0** | **89.9%** | **59.7** | **0%** |
| oracle (bound) | 92.7% | 99.5 | — |

Paired tests:

| A | B | accA | accB | A>B | B>A | p | tokens A−B (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| decision=completion | decision=prompt | 89.9% | 89.5% | 12 | 11 | 1.000 | −207.2 [−222.1, −193.5] |
| decision=completion | always | 89.9% | 89.5% | 13 | 12 | 1.000 | −204.5 [−220.1, −190.0] |
| decision=completion | adaptive prompt | 89.9% | 89.1% | 13 | 11 | 0.839 | −221.0 [−238.0, −205.4] |
| decision=completion | never | 89.9% | 71.4% | **46** | **0** | **0.000** | +8.3 [+5.7, +11.3] |

**No detectable accuracy cost for a 77% token reduction**, and **46–0 against the cost-matched
anchor** — the only significant accuracy result in the project, and one with no regressions at all.

The outputs are genuine. All 248 completions open with the empty marker and then answer: correct
multi-call output on `parallel`, correct prose abstention on `irrelevance`, well-formed JSON
throughout. Per-category against `never`: parallel 72.5 → 92.5, parallel_multiple 50.0 → 72.5,
irrelevance 37.5 → 93.8. Nothing degenerate is being exploited.

### What this is, and what it is not

**It is not a gate.** Think rate is 0%, not the oracle's 17.4%. The policy did not learn *when* to
reason; it learned not to need reasoning, and then reasoning became pure cost. Calling this
"adaptive thinking" would misdescribe it.

**The comparison to `never` is confounded and must be stated as such.** `never` is a *prompted*
policy and this is a *trained* one, so part of the 18.5-point gap is simply training on
correctness. The clean control is the other twelve trained runs: identical reward, identical
budget, and **not one of them saved any tokens** — 224 to 281, versus 59.7 here. That is what
attributes the result to the rollout design rather than to training in general.

**What is unconfounded** is the accuracy comparison against always-think at 4.4× lower cost, and
the 207-token gap against `decision=prompt`, which differs from it in exactly one respect.

**The collapse is real and limits the claim.** Once every rollout in a group stopped reasoning the
groups went degenerate, the gradient vanished, and the policy froze at step ~23. Seventy-six of the
hundred steps did nothing. lambda=2.0 does not find a gate; it finds an edge and stops there.

### The experiment this now makes obvious

lambda finally controls something, so a sweep over it has somewhere to land. lambda=2.0 gives 0%
think rate; the untrained model gives 97%. A gate, if one exists, is somewhere between. Five gate
runs at lambda ∈ {0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0} is ~90 minutes and is now the highest-value experiment
in the project — for the first time with a mechanism predicting that the knob will do something.

Watch for the collapse: if think rate hits 0 before step ~25 at every lambda, the objective has a
cliff rather than a curve, and that is its own finding.

## The sweep ran. It is a cliff.

All five gate runs land on the same policy. Not similar — the same.

```
policy               acc     tokens   think tok   think rate
gate λ=0.05        89.5%       61.0         0.0        0.0%
gate λ=0.25        89.5%       60.7         0.0        0.0%
gate λ=0.5         89.9%       60.9         0.0        0.0%
gate λ=1.0         89.5%       60.7         0.0        0.0%
gate λ=2.0         89.9%       59.7         0.0        0.0%
--
always             89.5%      264.1       190.9       98.4%
gate prompt (base) 89.9%      284.3       202.3       97.2%
ORACLE (bound)     92.7%       99.5           -           -
```

The endpoints are 40× apart in λ. McNemar on them: **0 versus 1 discordant items out of 248**,
p=1.000, +1.4 tokens [+0.3, +2.8]. Two policies trained under a fortyfold difference in the price
of a thinking token disagree about a single question.

They are not the same file. Comparing raw `output_text` between every pair of runs gives 91–93%
byte-identical, so the adapters genuinely differ; they just differ nowhere that matters. The λ knob
moves the weights and does not move the behaviour.

Training telemetry says the same thing from the other side. Every run starts around 0.44–0.47 think
rate — the forced half of each group — and hits exactly 0.00 within thirty steps: λ=1.0 at step 27,
λ=2.0 at step 23. After that the groups are degenerate, `std(reward)` is zero, and the remaining
seventy-odd steps are no-ops. The eval times gave it away before the scoring did: ~18 s for all five
sweep runs against ~47 s for any thinking policy.

**Why a cliff and not a curve.** The reward is `correctness + format − λ·think/768`. On a group
where correctness and format come out equal, which is most of them, the *only* variance is length,
so the standardised advantage is `±1` for direct-versus-thinking regardless of λ. λ sets nothing but
the sign, and the sign always points the same way. It only stops pointing that way when thinking
buys enough accuracy to flip the correctness term — and at 1.7B on BFCL single-turn, per the fp16
break-even table, it almost never does. So the argmin of the objective is 0% think rate for every λ
that is nonzero, and GRPO walks there directly. The intermediate think rate a curve would need does
not exist to be found.

Read the other way: this is the same λ-cancellation as Phase E, one level up. There the normaliser
divided λ out of the *magnitude*; here it divides λ out of the *outcome*. Fixing the rollout let λ
reach the gradient and did not let λ reach the answer.

**What survives, and it is the headline.** The 0%-think policy is not a degenerate one. 89.9%
against always-think's 89.5% at 4.4× fewer tokens, McNemar p=1.000 on 13-vs-12 discordant, and
−204.5 tokens/item [−220.1, −190.0]. Against the prompted `never` anchor at the same cost: 46 wins,
0 losses, p<0.001. **On this benchmark at this scale, chain-of-thought before a tool call is
purchasable — it costs 200 tokens an item and buys nothing measurable.** That is a real result about
tool use, and it is not the result the study set out to find.

**What died.** The adaptive gate. Not "we ran out of budget to find it" — the objective as written
has no interior optimum to find, and no value of λ produces one. Getting a gate needs a different
reward, not a different λ: something that prices *errors* asymmetrically enough that thinking pays
on the hard slice, or a per-category budget, or an entropy floor to stop the collapse at step 23.
Those are Phase G questions and none of them were tested here.

## What would come next, if there were time

The diagnosis points at a specific fix rather than a vague one. λ cancels because the normaliser is
computed over a set whose reward spread is purely length-driven. Two ways out:

- **Make the decision part of the completion.** Have the model emit an explicit gate token before
  reasoning, under one prompt for both modes. Then the direct branch is on-policy, pairing is
  legitimate, and the eval-time asymmetry disappears.
- **Take length out of the normaliser.** Standardise advantages on the correctness term alone and
  subtract the length penalty afterwards, so the length signal keeps its λ-proportional magnitude
  instead of being divided by it.

Both are single-day changes to `rewards/reward.py` and the rollout path. Neither was attempted here.

> **Superseded, same day.** The first one *was* attempted — that is `--gate-rollouts`, and it worked
> as described: the direct branch went on-policy and λ reached the gradient. It did not produce a
> gate, for the reason in "The sweep ran. It is a cliff." above. The second is still untried and is
> now less interesting: with a cliff in the objective, rescaling the length signal changes how fast
> the policy walks to 0%, not where it stops.
